Once a week, this sloth risks its life just to go to the toilet.

Up in the trees, it's almost impossible to catch. It moves so slowly that jaguars and eagles barely notice it, and its fur is so green it looks like part of the tree.

That green is algae, growing right in its fur. And living in there too are tiny moths. Dozens of them.

But a diet of leaves takes weeks to digest. So the sloth holds it in for about seven days.

Then it does something strange. Instead of going from the branch, it climbs all the way down to the ground. Slowly. Very slowly.

At the bottom, it digs a little hole with its tail, does a wiggly poop dance, and drops up to a third of its body weight in one go.

This is the most dangerous moment of its week. Down here, a jaguar can catch it.

So why risk it? Scientists think it's for the moths. They hop off, lay their eggs in the poop, and their babies grow up and fly back into the sloth's fur.

There, they feed the algae. And the sloth snacks on the algae.

Pretty gross. Pretty genius.
